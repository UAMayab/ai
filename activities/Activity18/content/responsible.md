## Rules for this activity

1. **Use your school accounts first.** With your school Google account, Gemini and Gemini Notebook are covered by Google Workspace for Education terms: Google says that data is *not reviewed by humans or used to train AI models*. With your school Microsoft account, Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat has *enterprise data protection*: check that you are signed in with your school account (Microsoft shows a shield icon) before you type.
2. **Never paste personal or confidential information** (your own or anyone else's): IDs, phone numbers, grades, health data, passwords, company secrets. Use the sample texts this app provides.
3. **Know the data settings of personal accounts.** Free consumer tools may use your conversations to improve their models unless you change a setting. ChatGPT: *Settings → Data controls → "Improve the model for everyone"*. Claude: the model-training choice in *Privacy Settings*. Google AI Studio's free tier: Google states that free-tier content is used to improve its products, so treat everything you type there as public.
4. **Respect age rules.** ChatGPT requires users to be 13 or older (under 18 with parental permission); Claude requires 18 or older.
5. **Never pay.** Every mission works on a free plan. If a tool asks for a credit card, skip it and use another option in the same mission.

## The bigger questions

- **Deepfakes and Content Credentials.** Generated images, voices, and videos can be indistinguishable from real ones. **Content Credentials** (the C2PA standard) attach a signed record of how a file was made; some tools, such as Adobe Firefly, add them automatically, and anyone can inspect a file at contentcredentials.org/verify. Missing credentials do not prove a file is fake, and valid credentials do not prove its content is true: they only show the record wasn't altered.
- **Copyright.** Who owns AI output, and whether training on copyrighted work is allowed, is still being decided in courts and laws around the world. Read each tool's terms before using its output commercially.
- **Bias.** Models learn the patterns, including the stereotypes, in their training data (Sessions 34–35). Check outputs about people especially carefully.
- **Energy.** The International Energy Agency (IEA) projects that electricity use by data centres roughly **doubles between 2024 and 2030**, with AI the main driver of the growth.
- **Regulation.** The EU's AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) already bans some uses (since February 2025) and sets rules for general-purpose models (since August 2025). Its transparency duties (telling people they are talking to an AI, labeling deepfakes) apply from **August 2026**. A 2026 amendment (the "Digital Omnibus") moved the rules for high-risk uses to December 2027 and August 2028. It matters outside Europe too: any company that offers AI in the EU must comply.
- **Your work, your name.** Using AI is a skill; passing off AI work as entirely your own is not. When your school, client, or employer asks, say what you used it for. This activity ends with exactly that: an AI-use disclosure.
