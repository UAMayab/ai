# Activity 3 — Domain Options
## Choose One Real-World AI Domain for Your Briefing

Each team picks **one** domain from the list below — first come, first served, so coordinate with your classmates so no two teams pick the same one. Every option below gives you the specific real application to describe (Session 4) and the specific real ethical/legal hook you must engage with (Session 5), so every team is graded against comparably concrete, real material.

> Unlike Activity 1's fictional Katún Manufactura, everything in this activity is **real** — real applications, real companies, real laws. Cite specific facts; don't generalize.

---

## 1. Healthcare — AI-Assisted Radiology

**The real application:** AI systems that analyze X-rays, CT scans, and MRIs to detect abnormalities (e.g. early cancer detection), giving radiologists a second set of "eyes" that can process large volumes of imaging data faster than manual review alone.

**The ethical/legal hook:** Patient privacy and data protection in medical AI. Engage with Mexico's **Federal Law for the Protection of Personal Data in Possession of Private Parties**, and the risk of algorithmic bias in diagnosis if training data isn't representative of the patient population being served.

---

## 2. Agriculture — AI + IoT Crop Monitoring

**The real application:** AI systems combined with Internet of Things sensors that monitor soil moisture, nutrient levels, and temperature, automatically triggering irrigation or climate-control responses.

**The ethical/legal hook:** Equitable access — smallholder farmers may not afford this technology, potentially widening the gap with large agribusiness. Also: who owns the farm data these sensors collect?

---

## 3. Energy — AI-Optimized Power Grids

**The real application:** Machine learning systems that analyze real-time energy usage patterns and historical data to predict grid failures, optimize electricity distribution, and reduce losses.

**The ethical/legal hook:** Transparency and accountability when an AI system's grid decision causes an outage or service disruption — who is responsible? Also environmental/sustainability tradeoffs of the AI infrastructure itself.

---

## 4. Manufacturing — Collaborative Robots (Cobots)

**The real application:** AI-equipped robots working alongside human operators on production lines (electronics assembly, automotive manufacturing), handling repetitive or dangerous tasks while humans handle more complex judgment calls.

**The ethical/legal hook:** Workplace/job-displacement ethics — the impact of automation on factory employment, and the employer's social responsibility toward affected workers (this directly echoes Session 5's "Ethics in AI in the Workplace" material).

---

## 5. Logistics & Transport — Route Optimization

**The real application:** AI-based route optimization for product delivery (the session content specifically cites Amazon's logistics systems), using algorithms like Dijkstra's to minimize delivery time and cost.

**The ethical/legal hook:** Ethics in transport and logistics — data privacy of drivers/customers whose location and delivery data is continuously collected, and fairness/working-condition concerns for gig-economy delivery workers whose routes and pace are set algorithmically.

---

## 6. Finance — AI Market Prediction

**The real application:** AI systems that process historical and real-time market data to forecast financial market fluctuations, used by financial institutions to guide investment decisions.

**The ethical/legal hook:** Algorithmic transparency and fairness in automated financial decision-making (e.g. algorithmic credit/lending decisions), and the international regulatory backdrop — reference the **EU's GDPR** as a model for data-handling accountability, since it's explicitly named in the session material as an international AI-adjacent legal framework.

---

## 7. Environmental Conservation — AI Wildlife Monitoring

**The real application:** Machine learning systems analyzing data from cameras, GPS trackers, and other surveillance equipment to monitor the location, behavior, and health of endangered species.

**The ethical/legal hook:** Sustainability and the AI system's own environmental footprint, balanced against its conservation benefit; also who controls access to sensitive location data on endangered species (poaching risk if such data leaked).

---

## 8. Cybersecurity — AI Threat Detection

**The real application:** Machine learning systems analyzing network traffic patterns in real time to detect intrusions, malware, and denial-of-service attacks before they cause damage.

**The ethical/legal hook:** Privacy tension — effective threat detection often requires broad monitoring of network traffic, which can itself infringe on the privacy of the very users it protects. Reference the **UN's work on AI ethics** as an example of an international body grappling with exactly this kind of dual-use tension.
