# Activity 3 — Presentation Guide
## How to Turn Your Chosen Domain Into Slides and Video

This guide breaks the assignment into slide-by-slide phases. Follow it in order.

---

## Before You Start

1. As a team, pick one domain from `A3_DomainOptions.md` and confirm no other team already claimed it.
2. Read your domain's entry completely — the real application and the ethical/legal hook are both already specified for you. Your job is to research and analyze them, not invent new ones.
3. Assign each team member a section to own (application research, ethics/legal research, slide design), while still reviewing every slide together.

> **Tip:** Everything in your deck should cite something specific and real — a named regulation, a named real-world use case, a specific mechanism (how the AI actually works in that domain) — not general statements that could apply to any AI system anywhere.

---

## Phase 1 — Title & Team Slide (1 slide)

Your domain name, your team name/members, and a one-line framing ("AI Applications & Ethics Briefing: [Your Domain]").

## Phase 2 — The Real Application (2–3 slides)

Describe the actual AI application named in your domain's brief. Explain, in plain terms, what the AI system actually does and how it creates value in that domain. Go beyond the one-paragraph description in `A3_DomainOptions.md` — find at least one additional real-world detail or example through your own research.

## Phase 3 — Ethical & Legal Analysis (2–3 slides)

Directly address the ethical/legal hook named in your domain's brief. Name the specific regulation, principle, or tension involved (e.g. GDPR, Mexico's data-protection law, workplace-displacement ethics) and explain why it matters specifically for this application — not ethics in general.

## Phase 4 — Recommendation (1 slide)

Given the benefits (Phase 2) and the risks (Phase 3), what's your team's overall verdict? Is this application, on balance, something society should encourage, regulate more tightly, or approach with caution — and under what safeguards?

## Phase 5 — Team Reflection (1 slide, spoken in the video)

Your own honest reflection — what surprised you, what you found harder to research than expected, or what changed your view while working on this.

## Phase 6 — Sources (1 slide)

List everything you consulted beyond the course session content and `A3_DomainOptions.md`.

---

## Recording the Video (English, 5–7 minutes)

- Split speaking parts so every team member talks.
- Speak to the camera, don't read slide text verbatim.
- Rehearse the transitions between speakers once before recording for real.
- Stay inside 5–7 minutes.

---

## Common Problems and How to Avoid Them

| Problem | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Application section reads like a generic "AI is used in X" summary | Name the specific mechanism/technique and cite a concrete real example beyond the one-liner in the domain brief |
| Ethics section discusses "AI ethics" in general | Tie directly to the specific regulation/tension named in your domain's brief |
| No real regulation or framework is named anywhere in the deck | At least one specific law, principle, or named international body (GDPR, Mexico's data-protection law, UN AI ethics work) must appear |
| Recommendation is vague ("AI has pros and cons") | State a clear verdict and the safeguards that would justify it |
| Reflection slide just restates the recommendation | Reflection must be personal, not a recap |
| One person talks for the whole video | Redistribute speaking parts before recording |
