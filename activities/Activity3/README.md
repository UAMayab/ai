# Activity Description

## AI 2027 — A Position Paper

**AI 2027** is a scenario-forecasting report published by the AI Futures Project (Daniel Kokotajlo, Eli Lifland, Romeo Dean, Thomas Larsen, and Scott Alexander). It presents a detailed, month-by-month speculative narrative of how artificial intelligence could develop between 2025 and 2027 and beyond — from increasingly capable autonomous AI agents, to a "superhuman coder" milestone that triggers a feedback loop of AI systems improving themselves, to an intense US-China race dynamic. The report then splits into two branching endings: a **"Race" ending**, where labs and governments push forward despite alignment warnings and humanity loses meaningful control, and a **"Slowdown" ending**, where a coalition forces a pause and oversight regime to verify AI systems are safe before continuing.

Read the full scenario at the official site before answering: https://ai-2027.com

Your task is to write a **personal position paper**: not a summary, but your own argued stance on the scenario, grounded in what you've learned in Sessions 4–6.

### Instructions

Write your position paper in Markdown, addressing all of the following prompts. Structure your paper with clear headings for each part. Remember: **elaborate your answers**. Well-argued reasoning, even if someone disagrees with your conclusion, earns full credit — a one-line opinion does not.

### Part 1 — Understanding the Scenario

1. In your own words, briefly describe the "superhuman coder" milestone and why the report treats it as the turning point of the whole scenario.
2. What is the core difference between the "Race" ending and the "Slowdown" ending? What decision, at what moment, is what actually separates the two paths?

### Part 2 — Take a Position

3. Which ending — Race or Slowdown — do you personally find **more plausible** as an outcome, independent of which one you'd prefer? Justify your answer using specific evidence or reasoning from the report, not just intuition.
4. Which ending do you think would be **more desirable** for humanity? If your answer differs from your answer to question 3, explain why plausibility and desirability diverge for you.
5. Do you think a coordinated "slowdown" (a pause with international oversight) is realistically achievable given the competitive dynamics described in the report? Why or why not?

### Part 3 — Connecting to Course Sessions

6. **(Session 4 — Applications of AI)** The report assumes AI will move from today's applications (medicine, finance, logistics, etc., as covered in Session 4) to autonomously conducting its own AI research. Pick one current application area from Session 4 and explain what it might look like if AI reached the "superhuman coder" level in that domain specifically.
7. **(Session 5 — Ethical issues)** Identify the **two ethical principles or issues from Session 5** (e.g., transparency, accountability, fairness, safety, global equity) that you think are most at risk in the "Race" scenario, and explain why.
8. **(Session 6 — Programming languages)** If AI systems become better than human programmers at writing and improving code, what do you think happens to the role of programming languages and human programmers as a discipline? Does the choice of language (Python, R, Java, etc., as covered in Session 6) still matter in that world? Defend your view.

### Part 4 — Personal Reflection

9. Has reading this report changed how you think about your own use of AI tools this semester, or your plans for a career that involves AI? Explain concretely — vague statements like "it made me think" will not receive full credit.
