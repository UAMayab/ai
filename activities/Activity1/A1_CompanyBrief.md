## 1. Company Overview

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Name | Katún Manufactura |
| Founded | 2004 |
| Industry | Precision-parts manufacturing (metal components for automotive and aerospace suppliers) |
| Employees | 340 |
| Location | Industrial Park Norte, Mérida, Yucatán, México |
| Clients | Three Tier-1 automotive suppliers and one aerospace subcontractor, all outside Mexico |
| Ownership | Family-owned, second generation, run by CEO **Rodrigo Balam** |

Katún Manufactura runs two production lines that stamp, machine, and finish metal parts to strict tolerance and safety specifications. Quality failures are costly: a defective batch shipped to an aerospace client can trigger a full-line audit and contract penalties.

---

## 2. The Situation

Two months ago, the CEO's leadership team was approached by an AI vendor, **NeuraLine Systems**, with a proposal to install:

1. **Predictive maintenance** — sensors and a machine learning model that predict when production-line machinery is likely to fail, before it actually breaks down.
2. **Visual quality inspection** — cameras and a computer vision model that scan finished parts on the line and flag defects automatically, instead of relying only on manual spot-checks.

NeuraLine's pitch materials repeatedly describe the system as "true artificial intelligence that thinks like your best inspector" and promise it will "learn and improve on its own with no need for retraining." The CEO has asked an outside team (**you**) to independently brief the board before signing anything.

### The Vendor's Pitch — Key Numbers

| Item | Value |
|------|-------|
| Upfront cost | $185,000 USD (hardware + software license) |
| Ongoing cost | $3,200 USD/month (support + model updates) |
| Promised install timeline | 6 weeks to "full autonomous operation" |
| Claimed defect-detection improvement | "Up to 95% fewer missed defects" |
| Claimed downtime reduction | "Up to 40% less unplanned downtime" |
| Contract term | 3-year minimum commitment |

---

## 3. Voices from the Board

You were given recordings of three short statements from last month's board meeting. Your briefing should speak to all three concerns.

> **Rodrigo Balam, CEO:**
> "NeuraLine says this is the same kind of AI that powers self-driving cars. If it's really that smart, it should just run itself and save us money everywhere. I want to know if that's realistic before we commit three years to it."

> **Elena Cetz, CFO:**
> "We've been burned before. Back in 2016 we bought a 'smart' inventory system that was supposed to run itself and predict everything automatically. It needed constant manual fixes for two years and never did what the brochure promised. I want to know what's actually different this time, if anything."

> **Marco Uc, Plant Floor Representative (speaking for the machine operators and line inspectors):**
> "Forty of our people do quality inspection and maintenance checks today. If a camera and a computer are going to 'flag defects automatically' and 'predict failures,' what happens to their jobs? Nobody on the floor has been told anything, and people are worried."

---

## 4. Financial & Strategic Stakes

- Katún's largest client has warned that a competitor plant in another country is already using automated inspection and has offered a faster defect-response guarantee. Katún risks losing part of that contract at renewal in 9 months if it cannot show a credible quality-improvement plan.
- The $185,000 upfront cost is roughly 15% of Katún's annual equipment budget — a real commitment, not a trivial trial purchase.
- The CEO has told the board he wants a recommendation, not just an explanation — the board meeting where your briefing will be shown is also the meeting where they vote on whether to sign the contract.

---

## 5. Your Assignment

The CEO's exact words to your team: *"Don't just tell me what AI is in general — tell me what NeuraLine is actually offering us, whether we should trust it based on what's happened before with 'smart' systems, what kind of people and skills we'd need to make it actually work, and what you personally would advise us to do."*

Your briefing (PowerPoint + video, see `README.md` and `A1_PresentationGuide.md`) must give Katún's board:

1. A plain-language classification of what NeuraLine is actually proposing (is this the same kind of AI as a self-driving car, as the CEO assumes? weak or strong AI?).
2. A historical lesson — grounded in the real history of AI's hype cycles and past overpromising — that speaks directly to the CFO's 2016 concern.
3. A clear list of the disciplines and expertise (beyond just "buying the software") Katún would need to make this work, responding to what the pitch leaves out.
4. A recommendation: sign, don't sign, or sign with conditions — and why.
5. Your team's own reflection on what surprised you most while researching this.
