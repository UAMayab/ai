# Activity 1 — Presentation Guide
## How to Turn the Katún Manufactura Brief Into Your Slides and Video

This guide breaks the assignment into slide-by-slide phases. Follow it in order — each phase builds on the one before it, and skipping ahead usually produces a deck that reads like a general AI summary instead of an actual briefing for Katún Manufactura.

---

## Before You Start

1. Read `A1_CompanyBrief.md` completely, as a team, before opening PowerPoint. Every fact you need is already in it — do not invent new numbers, quotes, or vendor claims.
2. Assign each of the three required content sections (below) to a different team member as the "owner" who researches and drafts it, even though the whole team reviews every slide together.
3. Decide now who will speak which parts in the video — this makes recording much faster later.

> **Tip:** Everything in your deck should be answerable with "because of something specific to Katún Manufactura's situation," not "because that's generally true about AI."

---

## Phase 1 — Title & Team Slide (1 slide)

Company name, your team name/members, and a one-line framing of your role ("AI Readiness Briefing prepared for the Katún Manufactura Board").

## Phase 2 — The Situation (1 slide)

Summarize the NeuraLine pitch and why the board asked for this briefing. This is context-setting only — save your analysis for the next three sections.

## Phase 3 — Section 1: What Is NeuraLine Actually Offering? (1–2 slides)

Directly answer the CEO's assumption that this is "the same kind of AI that powers self-driving cars." Classify predictive maintenance and visual quality inspection using the weak-AI/strong-AI distinction from Session 1. Be specific about what the system can and cannot plausibly do on its own.

## Phase 4 — Section 2: The Historical Lesson (1–2 slides)

Respond directly to CFO Elena Cetz's 2016 concern. Pick one moment or pattern from AI's history (e.g., the expert-systems era, an AI winter, or a specific figure's over-optimistic prediction that didn't hold up) and explain what it should teach the board about trusting vendor promises like "learns and improves on its own with no need for retraining."

## Phase 5 — Section 3: What Would This Actually Take? (1–2 slides)

Name the disciplines and expertise Katún would need beyond the software purchase itself (e.g., data/statistics know-how to validate the model's claims, domain expertise from the plant-floor inspectors NeuraLine would supposedly replace, ethical/workforce planning for Marco Uc's 40 workers). This section should make it clear "buying an AI system" is not the same as "having a working AI system."

## Phase 6 — Recommendation (1 slide)

State clearly: sign, don't sign, or sign with conditions — and the top 2-3 reasons why, tying back to Sections 1-3.

## Phase 7 — Team Reflection (1 slide, spoken in the video)

Not a summary of the brief — your own honest reflection on what surprised you, what you found harder than expected, or what changed your mind while researching this.

## Phase 8 — Sources (1 slide)

List anything you consulted beyond the course sessions and the company brief.

---

## Recording the Video (English, 5–7 minutes)

- Split speaking parts so every team member talks — a video where one person reads the whole deck loses points under "all members participate."
- Speak to the camera/audience, not by reading slide text word-for-word. Slides should support what you say, not replace it.
- Practice the transitions between speakers once before recording — dead air between handoffs is the easiest thing to fix with one rehearsal.
- Stay inside 5–7 minutes. Going over is better than a rushed under-5-minute video, but neither is ideal — plan your section lengths before recording.

---

## Common Problems and How to Avoid Them

| Problem | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Deck reads like a general "history of AI" summary | Every slide should reference Katún, NeuraLine, or one of the three named board members specifically |
| Section 1 doesn't actually classify NeuraLine's system | Explicitly say "weak AI" or "not strong AI" and explain why in Katún's context |
| Section 2 is a random history fact with no connection to the CFO's concern | Tie the historical lesson directly back to Elena Cetz's quote about the 2016 system |
| Section 3 only lists "buy software" | Name specific expertise/roles Katún would need to hire, train, or consult |
| Reflection slide just restates the recommendation | Reflection must be personal ("what surprised us") not a recap of the briefing |
| One person talks for the whole video | Redistribute speaking parts before recording, not after |
